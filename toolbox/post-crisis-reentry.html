Appendix 5.7 · Return-to-sport protocol

Post-Crisis Re-Entry Protocol

A three-phase protocol for safely reintegrating an athlete into team activities after a mental health crisis and treatment.

Supports Guideline 07 — Emergency Action Planning

Read the guideline

Purpose

This protocol provides guidance for safely and supportively reintegrating athletes into team activities following a mental health crisis and treatment. The goal is to prioritize the athlete's well-being while facilitating a smooth return to sport when appropriate.

Core principles

  • Return is individualized and athlete-centered
  • Medical or mental health clearance is required
  • Return is gradual and flexible
  • Communication is collaborative
  • Privacy and dignity are maintained
  • Ongoing support is provided

Phase 1 · Pre-return planning

Timeline: before the athlete returns to any team activity.

Step 1 — Obtain medical / mental health clearance

  • Obtain written clearance from the treating mental health provider.
  • Review any recommended accommodations or limitations.
  • Obtain clearance from the team physician or athletic trainer if applicable.
  • Document the clearance in the athlete's file.

The clearance letter should include: confirmation the athlete is ready to return to sport participation · any activity restrictions or modifications · recommended support or accommodations · the follow-up treatment plan · emergency contact information for the treatment provider.

Step 2 — Hold a re-entry meeting

Participants: athlete, parent/guardian (if a minor), athletic director or coach, athletic trainer or school counselor, and optionally the mental health provider with the athlete's consent.

Agenda

  • Welcome the athlete back and express support.
  • Review the clearance and any restrictions.
  • Discuss the athlete's own goals and concerns about returning.
  • Develop the individualized return-to-play plan (Phase 2).
  • Identify a point person for ongoing check-ins.
  • Review confidentiality and information sharing with the team.
  • Establish communication protocols and schedule follow-up meetings.

Step 3 — Prepare the team, as appropriate

Critical: obtain the athlete’s permission — and, for a minor, their family’s — before sharing any information with teammates. Agree the actual wording with them rather than deciding it afterwards. Where the crisis involved suicidal behavior, follow safe messaging: no method, no location, and crisis line numbers alongside whatever is said.

  • Discuss with the athlete what, if anything, they want teammates to know.
  • Prepare a simple, respectful statement if the athlete agrees to brief the team.
  • Address speculation or rumors privately before the athlete returns.
  • Remind the team about confidentiality and respect.

Sample team statement, with the athlete's approval:

"[Athlete name] has been away dealing with some personal health matters. They're ready to return and we're excited to have them back. Let's make sure we support them as they get back into the rhythm of practice and competition. If you have questions, please come talk to me rather than asking [athlete] directly."

Phase 2 · Gradual return to play

Timeline: typically the first 2–4 weeks, flexible based on individual needs.

Progress through stages only when the athlete is ready and shows no increased symptoms.

If symptoms worsen at any stage, pause and consult the mental health provider before proceeding. It is okay to slow down or move backward through the stages.

Phase 3 · Ongoing support and monitoring

Timeline: week 3 onward.

Regular check-ins

Establish a consistent schedule with the athlete's designated support person — coach, athletic trainer or counselor.

  • Weeks 1–2: daily brief check-ins, around five minutes
  • Weeks 3–6: two or three times per week
  • Week 7 onward: weekly or bi-weekly

Check-in topics

  • How is the return going? Any challenges?
  • How are you managing stress and workload?
  • Are you keeping up with the treatment plan?
  • How are relationships with teammates?
  • Do you need any accommodations or adjustments?
  • Any warning signs returning?

Potential accommodations

  • Modified practice schedule, such as skipping early morning workouts
  • Flexible attendance for therapy appointments
  • Reduced travel for away competitions initially
  • Break time during practice if needed
  • A quiet space available before or after practice
  • Modified performance expectations during the transition
  • A named point person for concerns or difficulties

Warning signs of relapse

  • Return of previous symptoms
  • Increased isolation or withdrawal
  • Missed therapy appointments or medication non-compliance
  • Sudden changes in performance or behavior
  • Increased conflict with teammates or coaches
  • Expressions of overwhelm or inability to cope

If warning signs appear: contact the athlete's mental health provider immediately and consider adjusting the return-to-play plan.

Communication guidelines for coaches

What to do

  • Express genuine welcome and support
  • Focus on the athlete as a whole person, not their performance
  • Be patient with the readjustment process
  • Check in regularly, but don't hover or single them out
  • Respect their privacy and boundaries
  • Acknowledge their courage in seeking help
  • Maintain a normal coaching relationship while being sensitive

What not to do

  • Ask for details about what happened during their absence
  • Share information with others without permission
  • Make assumptions about what they need
  • Treat them differently in ways that draw unwanted attention
  • Rush them through the return process
  • Make their mental health the focus of every interaction
  • Use stigmatizing language or make jokes

Helpful phrases

"It's great to have you back. How are you feeling about being here?"

"Let me know if you need anything, or if something doesn't feel right."

"There's no rush — we'll go at your pace."

"How can I best support you right now?"

"I'm glad you're taking care of yourself."

Documentation and review

Required documentation

  • Medical or mental health clearance letter
  • Re-entry meeting notes and return-to-play plan
  • Accommodation plan, if applicable
  • Check-in log with dates and brief notes
  • Stage progression tracking
  • Any concerns or modifications made during the process

30-day review meeting

Schedule a review approximately 30 days after return.

  • How is the return going overall?
  • Are current accommodations working?
  • Any adjustments needed to the support plan?
  • Is the athlete maintaining their treatment plan?
  • Are there any ongoing concerns?
  • What's going well that we should continue?
