The 5 D's of bystander intervention

DIRECT
Address it head-on		Speak directly to the person engaging in the concerning behavior.
DISTRACT
Interrupt the situation		Create an interruption that stops the behavior without confronting it.
DELEGATE
Get help from others		Report to someone with authority or expertise.
DELAY
Check in after the fact		Follow up with the athlete or person to check on their well-being.
DOCUMENT
Record what you saw		Date, time, location, individuals, behavior, witnesses.

You don't need certainty — just reasonable concern.

Quick intervention scripts

Closed-door meeting — "Our policy requires one-on-one meetings to be observable. Let's move to a room with windows, or keep the door open."

Late-night texting — "Our communication policy requires messages to athletes under 18 to copy parents/guardians and occur during appropriate hours."

Inappropriate physical contact — "Let's stick to high-fives, fist bumps and side hugs, as set out in our code of conduct."

Verbal abuse — "That language crosses the line. We need to give feedback without name-calling or humiliation."

Checking on an athlete — "Are you okay? Is there anything you want to talk about? You can always come to me if something makes you uncomfortable."

Warning signs to watch for

Boundary violations

  • Excessive one-on-one time with individual athletes
  • Private communications that exclude parents/guardians
  • Meetings behind closed doors without visibility
  • Inappropriate physical contact or comments

Special relationships

  • Singling out individual athletes for special treatment
  • Gift-giving to specific athletes
  • Social activities outside team functions
  • Favoritism or creating dependency

Emotional / verbal abuse

  • Name-calling, belittling or humiliation
  • Yelling, threats or intimidation
  • Public shaming or isolation
  • Excessive criticism unrelated to performance

Secrecy and isolation

  • Asking athletes to keep interactions secret
  • Isolating athletes from teammates or families
  • Meeting in locations away from others
  • Resistance to supervision or transparency

If you see something, say something. Trust your instincts.
