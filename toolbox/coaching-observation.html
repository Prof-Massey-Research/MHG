Appendix 5.2 · Observation tool

Coaching Climate Observation Form

A peer or mentor watches one session and tallies what actually happens, so the debrief afterwards rests on what was seen rather than what either person remembers. Pairs with the Coach Self-Reflection Journal: the coach completes that, the observer completes this, and the two are compared.

Supports Guideline 03 — Coach Development for Supportive Coaching

Read the guideline

Before you use this

This is a development tool, not an evaluation instrument. Agree with the coach beforehand that it is being used, who sees it, and that it feeds a conversation rather than a personnel decision. An observation form used as a performance review will produce careful coaching for one session and nothing else.

Part 1 · Behavior tally

Mark a tally each time you observe the behavior. Count what happens, not what you think was meant. These categories match Part 1 of the Coach Self-Reflection Journal, so the coach’s own estimates and your counts can be set side by side.

Responses to good performance

BehaviorTallyTotal
Reinforcement
Positive, supportive reaction to good performance or effort. “Great job staying with that play.”
Non-reinforcement
No response when an athlete does something well.

Responses to mistakes

BehaviorTallyTotal
Mistake-contingent encouragement
“That’s okay, shake it off.” “No worries, next play.”
Mistake-contingent technical instruction
“Try stepping with your opposite foot next time.”
Punishment
Negative reaction, criticism, yelling. “How many times do I have to tell you?”
Punitive technical instruction
Instruction delivered in a hostile or harsh tone.
Ignoring mistakes
No response at all.

Everything else

BehaviorTallyTotal
Keeping control
Responses intended to restore or maintain order.
General technical instruction
Instruction unrelated to a specific mistake.
General encouragement
Encouragement not tied to a particular play.
Organization
Administrative direction — setting drills, assigning positions.
General communication
Interaction unrelated to the task at hand.

Part 2 · Climate

Rate what the session emphasised. 1 = not at all, 4 = consistently.

Observation1234
Effort and improvement were recognised, not only outcomes
Mistakes were treated as part of learning
Athletes were given real choices within the session
Challenge was pitched so athletes could succeed with effort
Every athlete was spoken to by name, not only the strongest
Athletes appeared comfortable asking questions or admitting difficulty

Part 3 · Debrief notes

What worked. Something specific the coach did that you would want other coaches to see.

One thing to try next session. One, not a list.

What the coach said when you compared notes. Where did their self-assessment and your tally differ?

Using this form

  • Observe a whole session where possible. Short samples exaggerate whatever happened to occur.
  • Sit where you can hear, and stay out of the session.
  • Tally during the session; rate the climate immediately afterwards while it is fresh.
  • Debrief within a day, starting with what worked.
  • Observe the same coach more than once. A single session is a snapshot, not a pattern.

Behavior categories adapted from the coaching behavior research underpinning Coach Effectiveness Training (Smith & Smoll), as used in the Coach Self-Reflection Journal.
