Appendix 5.4 · Role description

Mental Health Point Person

A complete role description for the person who carries mental health accountability in your organization — including what the role is not.

Supports Guideline 04 — Mental Health Literacy

Read the guideline

Purpose of this role

The Mental Health Point Person serves as the organization's champion for athlete mental wellness. This person ensures mental health is prioritized, staff are equipped with knowledge and resources, and athletes have clear pathways to support. The role creates accountability and continuity for mental health initiatives.

Who can fill this role?

  • Athletic director or program director
  • School counselor, if the program is school-based
  • Athletic trainer with mental health training
  • Head coach with Mental Health First Aid certification
  • Volunteer with a professional mental health background
  • Mental performance consultant, where available

For small organizations: one person can wear multiple hats. The key is having someone designated and accountable for mental health — even if it is only 2–4 hours per month.

Core responsibilities

1. Be the go-to resource

  • Serve as the primary contact for mental health questions and concerns.
  • Be available to coaches, staff, athletes and parents/guardians for consultation.
  • Provide guidance on how to handle situations that arise — not provide therapy.

2. Maintain knowledge of resources

  • Keep an updated list of local mental health resources: therapists, crisis services, support groups.
  • Know the organization's mental health guidelines and emergency action plans.
  • Stay informed about best practice in youth sport mental health.

3. Coordinate training

  • Organize mental health literacy training for coaches and volunteers.
  • Track who has completed training and follow up with those who have not.
  • Share relevant articles, resources or updates with staff.

4. Build relationships with helpers

  • Develop connections with local school counselors, therapists and mental health agencies.
  • Know who to call for consultation when difficult situations arise.
  • Create a warm-handoff process so athletes feel supported when referred to outside help.

5. Champion mental health culture

  • Normalize conversations about mental health and reduce stigma.
  • Advocate for athlete well-being in program decisions and policies.
  • Share success stories, with permission, of athletes who sought help and thrived.

6. Monitor and communicate

  • Collect feedback from athlete well-being surveys and share results with leadership.
  • Report to leadership on initiatives and concerns, quarterly or as needed.
  • Document trends or patterns that need attention, without violating confidentiality.

Suggested qualifications

Required

  • Commitment to athlete well-being and mental health
  • Strong interpersonal and communication skills
  • Ability to maintain confidentiality
  • Willingness to learn about mental health basics

Strongly preferred

  • Mental Health First Aid certification, or completion within the first six months
  • Previous training in mental health literacy
  • Experience working with youth in sport or education settings

Helpful but not required

  • Professional mental health background — counseling, social work, psychology
  • Lived experience with mental health challenges
  • Existing connections to local mental health providers

What this role is not

The Mental Health Point Person is not:

  • A therapist or counselor providing treatment
  • The only person responsible for athlete mental health — everyone plays a role
  • Required to be available 24/7 for crisis response
  • Expected to have all the answers or solve every problem

This person is a connector, advocate and culture-builder — not a treatment provider.

Organizational support needed

  • Clear designation and communication — every coach, staff member, athlete and family knows who this person is and how to reach them.
  • Protected time — this cannot be an add-on with no time allocated. Even two hours a month should be officially designated.
  • Budget for training — cover the cost of Mental Health First Aid certification (approximately $100–200) if not already certified.
  • Access to consultation — a connection to a licensed mental health professional for complex situations.
  • Leadership backing — visible support from organizational leadership.

Getting started in this role

Month 1

  • Introduce yourself and your role to all staff, and share your contact information.
  • Create a list of local mental health resources — therapists, crisis lines, support groups.
  • Review your organization's mental health guidelines, if they exist.

Months 2–3

  • Complete Mental Health First Aid training if not already certified.
  • Schedule mental health literacy training for coaches and staff.
  • Meet with two or three local mental health providers to establish relationships.

Ongoing

  • Be available for questions and consultations.
  • Send quarterly updates to organizational leadership.
  • Continue learning through articles, webinars and conferences.
