Appendix 5.5 · One-pager

Triage vs. Therapy

A single-page reminder of where a coach's role begins and ends. Print it and put it where staff will see it.

Supports Guideline 05 — Crisis Response Training

Read the guideline

You are not a therapist — and that's okay.

Your role is to recognize, support and connect. Not to diagnose or treat.

Think of it like physical first aid

When an athlete breaks their leg during practice, you don't try to set the bone and cast it yourself. You:

  • Recognize the injury is serious
  • Keep the athlete calm and safe
  • Call for professional medical help
  • Stay with them until help arrives
  • Follow up to see how recovery is going

Mental Health First Aid works the same way. You provide immediate support and get the athlete connected to professionals who can provide treatment.

Where the line sits

Your job — triage

  • Notice changes in mood, behavior or performance
  • Start the conversation and listen without judgment
  • Validate what the athlete is experiencing
  • Keep them safe in the moment
  • Connect them to the right professional
  • Tell parents/guardians and your point person
  • Document what you observed and what you did
  • Follow up — check the handoff landed

Not your job — treatment

  • Diagnosing a condition
  • Providing ongoing counseling or therapy
  • Advising on medication
  • Being the athlete's confidant in place of a professional
  • Promising to keep safety concerns secret
  • Being available 24/7
  • Solving the underlying problem
  • Carrying it alone

The handoff

Staff are first responders, not the treatment team. The job is three words: recognize, respond, refer.

Protecting yourself

Responding to an athlete in distress takes something out of you. Organizations should keep overall workload manageable, hold a debrief as soon as feasible after any challenging situation, and give staff access to consultation with a mental health professional. If you have just handled something difficult, tell someone. That is part of the protocol, not a sign you couldn't cope.
