Appendix 5.3 · Feedback instrument

End-of-Season Developmental Experience Survey

An anonymous survey capturing the athlete and parent/guardian experience, used to evaluate coaches against developmental goals rather than results.

Supports Guideline 02 — Long-Term Athlete Development

Read the guideline

Why run this survey

An end-of-season survey keeps a program focused on long-term athlete growth rather than short-term results. It prevents coaches from drifting back into win-first habits, creates a formalized, transparent system for evaluating coaches against stated developmental goals, and highlights areas for ongoing coach education. Incorporating anonymized feedback from parents/guardians and athletes reveals whether the program's intended goals are actually translating into the real-world experience of participants.

Instructions to include with the survey

This survey is 100% anonymous. Your feedback will be combined with responses from the entire team and used by our program directors to help our coaches grow and to ensure we are meeting our developmental goals.

Part 1 · The athlete experience

To be completed by, or with, the athlete. Rate each statement based on your experience with your coach this season.

Scale: 1 = Never · 2 = Sometimes · 3 = Most of the time · 4 = Always

Optional additions for athlete well-being

Where you are also using this survey to meet Guideline 04, consider adding items on: overall team climate and psychological safety · stress related to sport participation · perceptions of coach support and communication · awareness of and access to mental health resources · experiences of burnout or overtraining · suggestions for improving athlete well-being.

After the survey

  • Analyze results and share key findings in aggregate with staff and athletes, protecting individual confidentiality.
  • Turn findings into a written action plan with named owners.
  • Close the loop — tell athletes and families what will change, and then do it. This is the step most programs skip.
  • For athletes under 13, use age-appropriate methods instead: brief check-in questions, emoji-based mood tracking, or facilitated small group discussion.
