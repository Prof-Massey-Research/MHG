Appendix 5.3 · Public statement

Youth Sport Developmental Charter

A one-page public commitment stating that your program prioritizes long-term growth over short-term competitive outcomes. Publish it where families can see it.

Supports Guideline 02 — Long-Term Athlete Development

Read the guideline

How to use this

This charter is meant to be public. Publish it on your website, in registration materials and on social media — not filed in a policy binder. Its value comes from families and coaches having read it before the season, so that expectations are set rather than defended.

[Organization Name] Youth Sport Developmental Charter

Our core philosophy

At [Organization Name], we prioritize the long-term physical, psychological and social growth of our athletes over short-term competitive outcomes.

The current youth sports culture often pushes children to specialize early, train like adults, and focus exclusively on winning. Science tells us this leads to burnout, overuse injuries, and athletes quitting sport entirely. We are choosing a better way. We are committed to a developmentally calibrated approach that builds foundational athletic skills, fosters a genuine love for the game, and develops better humans — not just better players.

Our commitments to our athletes

Development over the scoreboard

We define success by skill acquisition, effort and personal improvement — not by weekend win–loss records. We guarantee that playing time, coaching attention and resources in our foundational stages are dedicated to developing all players, not just early bloomers.

Biological pacing, not adult schedules

We strictly align our practice-to-game ratios, weekly time commitments and travel schedules to the biological and psychological readiness of our athletes. We mandate rest days to protect athletes from physical and mental exhaustion.

Championing the multi-sport athlete

We actively encourage our athletes to sample and participate in other sports. We will never penalize a player for missing off-season or optional training to play another sport — cross-training builds superior, injury-resistant athletes.

Teaching physical literacy first

Before we demand complex, sport-specific tactics, we ensure our athletes learn fundamental movement skills: agility, balance, coordination and speed.

Proactive inclusion

We are committed to removing physical, economic and cultural barriers to participation. Our programming is designed to be accessible and welcoming to everyone, regardless of background or starting skill level.
