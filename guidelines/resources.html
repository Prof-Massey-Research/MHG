Guideline 06

Locating Resources & Services

Youth sport programs document where mental health resources and services can be located.

Why this matters

Programs must actively bridge the gap between need and care. That means maintaining an up-to-date directory of mental health services — crisis hotlines, local counseling centers, tele-health options — and making sure the information is visible to everyone who might need it.

A systematic review and meta-synthesis of mental health policy and position statements from organizations including the NCAA, IOC and multiple sport psychology and sport science bodies found that explicit referral criteria, a built referral network, and a professional support community are key components of protecting athlete mental health.

Implementation protocol

  1. Identify local mental health resources

    Relationships with local providers, crisis centers and emergency services should exist before a crisis occurs — not be assembled during one.

    Implementation list
    • Maintain an updated list of local crisis hotlines, mobile crisis teams, licensed mental health professionals willing to consult, hospitals with psychiatric emergency services, and community-based crisis response programs.
    • Identify resources with a particular focus on young people.
    • For programs that travel, develop protocols for identifying resources in visiting locations.

  2. Make the information easily accessible to families and athletes

    A directory that no one can find does no work. Resources need to be visible in the places athletes actually are, and available discreetly.

    Implementation list
    • Publish the directory on the program website with a persistent, obvious entry point.
    • Post physical flyers in locker rooms, restrooms and other private spaces where a young person can read them without being watched.
    • Include the directory in preseason packets for parents/guardians and in coach onboarding materials.
    • Review contact details at least annually — numbers and providers change.

National crisis & support lines

  • 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline — call or text 988 · 988lifeline.org
  • Crisis Text Line — text HOME or HELP to 741741 · free 24/7 crisis counseling via text
  • Trevor Lifeline (LGBTQ+) — 1-866-488-7386 · thetrevorproject.org
  • SAMHSA National Helpline — 1-800-662-HELP (4357) · treatment and recovery support · samhsa.gov
  • NAMI Helpline — 1-800-950-6264 · nami.org
  • National Alliance for Eating Disorders — 1-800-931-2237
  • RAINN (National Sexual Assault Hotline) — call 1-800-656-HOPE (4673), text HOPE to 64673 · rainn.org/hotline
  • Samaritans Confidential Hotline — 1-212-673-3000 · samaritanshope.org
