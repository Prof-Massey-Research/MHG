Mental health belongs in every youth sport program.

Youth sport reaches more than 27 million young people a year across every community, culture, and background, yet there is no national standard for the quality of that experience. These seven guidelines — developed in conjunction with the top experts in youth sport and mental health in the USA, and using an evidence-based protocol — define what a psychologically healthy program looks like, and how to enhance that experience for all young people.

The seven guidelines

What a psychologically healthy program does

Each guideline pairs a plain-language standard with the evidence behind it and an implementation protocol. Start with a foundational level across all seven, then go deeper.

Guideline What it asks of a program
01 Safeguarding & Abuse Prevention Youth sport programs comply with best practices in safeguarding and abuse prevention. → 02 Alignment with Long-Term Athlete Development Youth sport programs are aligned to long-term athlete development models. → 03 Coach Development for Supportive Coaching Youth sport programs require and support their coaches to engage in ongoing coach development in methods that promote safe and supportive coaching. → 04 Mental Health Literacy Training Youth sport programs require and support their key actors to engage in mental health literacy training. → 05 Mental Health Crisis Response Training Youth sport programs require and support mental health crisis response training for organizational leaders who work directly with young people. → 06 Locating Resources & Services Youth sport programs document where mental health resources and services can be located. → 07 Mental Health Emergency Action Planning Youth sport programs have an emergency action plan that includes mental health. →

How to use this

Implementation is a continuum

No program is expected to do everything at once. The recommended approach is to reach a foundational level across all seven guidelines first, then progressively deepen. Most organizations will find they are already strong in some areas — start by finding those. Because youth sport settings vary widely and athletes themselves bring diverse individual identities, backgrounds, and lived experiences, implementation must adapt to both your program's setting structure and your community's unique demographic and cultural context.

Starting point

Foundational

The entry point. The basic protection or plan that should exist before a season starts.

No longer a one-off. Responsibility is named, training happens, the work repeats reliably.

Embedded in the annual cycle — audited, surveyed or rehearsed on a schedule.

Shapes how the organization runs: evaluation, hiring, referrals and operations reflect it.
