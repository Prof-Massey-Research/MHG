Mental health belongs in every youth sport program.

Youth sport reaches more than 27 million young people a year, yet there is no national standard for the quality of that experience. These seven guidelines — developed in conjunction with the top experts in youth sport and mental health in the country, using an evidence-based protocol — define what a psychologically healthy program looks like, and how to enhance the sport experience for young people.

The seven guidelines

What a psychologically healthy program does

Each guideline pairs a plain-language standard with the evidence behind it and an implementation protocol. Start with a foundational level across all seven, then go deeper.

Guideline What it asks of a program
01 Safeguarding & Abuse Prevention Youth sport programs comply with best practices in safeguarding. → 02 Alignment with Long-Term Athlete Development Youth sport programs are aligned to long-term athlete development models. → 03 Coach Development for Safe Environments Youth sport programs require and support their coaches to engage in ongoing coach development in methods that promote safe and supportive coaching. → 04 Mental Health Literacy Training Youth sport programs require and support their key actors to engage in mental health literacy training. → 05 Mental Health Crisis Response Training Youth sport programs require and support mental health crisis response training (e.g., Mental Health First Aid) for all paid staff who work directly with young people. → 06 Locating Resources & Services Youth sport programs document where mental health resources and services can be located. → 07 Mental Health Emergency Action Planning Youth sport programs have an emergency action plan that includes mental health. →

How to use this

Implementation is a continuum, not a pass/fail test

No program is expected to do everything at once. The recommended approach is to reach a foundational level across all seven guidelines first, then progressively deepen. Most organizations will find they are already strong in some areas — start by finding those.

Stage 01

Foundational

The entry point. The basic protection or plan that should exist before a season starts.

Stage 02

Developing

No longer a one-off. Responsibility is named, training happens, the work repeats reliably.

Stage 03

Established

Embedded in the annual cycle — audited, surveyed or rehearsed on a schedule.

Stage 04

Advanced

Shapes how the organization runs: evaluation, hiring, referrals and operations reflect it.

Stage 05

Long-term sustainability

Survives staff turnover and keeps improving. A model others can learn from.

Guideline development

How the guidelines were made

A four-part process, designed so the result would be scientifically sound and practical enough to adopt. Panelists reached consensus on seven guidelines.

  1. 01

    Rapid review of the scientific literature, following Cochrane protocol, on sport and youth mental health in the U.S.

  2. 02

    Focus groups with 60+ athletes, parents/guardians, coaches and organizational leaders.

  3. 03

    A four-round Delphi study with 22 experts, rating each candidate guideline on importance and feasibility.

  4. 04

    A two-day in-person consensus workshop with 16 panelists, with repeated votes and line-by-line wording debate.
